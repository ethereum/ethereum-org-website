# Build a durable Ethereum project

**Route:** `/founders`

**Page purpose:** Give founders a clear, public starting point for turning an
idea into a responsible Ethereum project. It should complement—not duplicate—
developer documentation, the Ecosystem Support Program, or third-party
accelerators. The page should not imply that ethereum.org or the Ethereum
Foundation endorses a project, a provider, or a funding decision.

## Content direction

The current page is valuable as a directory of support programmes, but it
starts with funding and external brands. The revised page should first help a
founder make good choices for users and the ecosystem, then offer a transparent
way to find relevant support. This makes Founders a genuine third path in the
Organizations hub: **Enterprise** evaluates an established organization’s
needs; **Institutions** considers public-interest infrastructure; **Founders**
helps a new project begin responsibly and remain accountable as it grows.

## Start where you are

### Validate the problem

Identify who needs to coordinate, what they need to verify or settle, and why
a shared, neutral system is better than one organization running a database.
If a single trusted operator is sufficient, conventional software may be the
better choice.

### Build a small, reversible first version

Learn the basics, use test networks, and keep the first deployment narrow.
Make it possible for users and your own team to leave, migrate, or recover when
an assumption proves wrong.

### Design the trust model

Be explicit about who can upgrade contracts, control keys, change parameters,
or pause the system. Choose the smallest trust boundary your users can
reasonably understand and verify.

### Prepare to operate

Security, accessibility, privacy, support, incident response, and governance
are product work—not items to add after launch. Plan how the project will be
maintained before relying on other people’s funds, data, or identity.

## Build for users and the long term

### Put users before lock-in

Give people understandable choices and credible ways to exit. Avoid making a
wallet, provider, bridge, or interface an unnecessary permanent dependency.

### Prefer open standards and interfaces

Use established Ethereum standards when they fit. Document your interfaces and
assumptions so users, integrators, and future maintainers can verify and
replace parts of the system.

### Treat privacy and security as design constraints

Public blockchain activity is observable by default. Decide what should be
public, what must stay confidential, and what data should never be collected.
Use independent review, staged releases, and clearly communicated risks where
the consequences justify them.

### Sustain what you ship

Choose governance and funding models that make the project legible to its
users. If your project becomes useful infrastructure, its maintenance burden
should be visible—not shifted silently onto its users or the wider ecosystem.

## From idea to stewardship

1. **Define** — Write the user problem, participants, and trust assumptions.
2. **Prototype** — Build the smallest version that tests those assumptions.
3. **Learn** — Test with real users, publish what changed, and remove what is
   not needed.
4. **Launch responsibly** — Set clear limits, security practices, support
   routes, and an incident plan.
5. **Steward** — Maintain the project, improve its decentralization where it
   matters, and remain accountable to the people who depend on it.

## A guide, not a gatekeeper

ethereum.org provides public educational information. It does not provide
investment, legal, tax, security, or grant advice; rank commercial providers;
or determine eligibility for external programmes. Always verify an
opportunity’s current terms directly with its operator.

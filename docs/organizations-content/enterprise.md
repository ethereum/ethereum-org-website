## Hero

**H1:** Ethereum for enterprises

Ethereum is public infrastructure that no company owns and no company can
switch off. That can give an organization a more open, interoperable, and
verifiable foundation for coordination - but it also changes how the
organization manages privacy, security, operations, and risk.

This hub explains what Ethereum provides, what it does not provide, and how to
evaluate whether it fits your organization.

**Primary CTA:** Understand the architecture
**Secondary CTA:** Evaluate Ethereum

## Start with your question

- **What is structurally different about Ethereum?** Explore open
  infrastructure, CROPS, and trust boundaries.
- **What could our organization build?** Explore digital assets, market
  infrastructure, credentials, and shared records.
- **Where should it run?** Compare Mainnet, L2s, tailored environments, and a
  conventional system.
- **How do we protect data and users?** Explore confidential systems and
  operational resilience.

## A foundation, not a finished product

Ethereum does not provide legal compliance, custody, incident response, or a
business model. Public activity is observable unless privacy is designed in.
Smart contracts, keys, bridges, providers, and upgrades all introduce their own
risks. Responsible adoption starts with one bounded use case and a clear exit
path for users and the organization.

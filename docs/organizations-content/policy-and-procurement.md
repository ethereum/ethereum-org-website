# Procuring open digital infrastructure

**Route:** `/institutions/policy-and-procurement`

## Hero

**H1:** Procuring open digital infrastructure

Procurement should assess more than a provider’s present capabilities. For
long-lived public infrastructure, assess whether people and institutions can
inspect, govern, replace, and recover the systems they depend on.

## Questions for a procurement process

### Sovereignty and dependency

Who operates the critical components? What happens if a supplier, integrator,
or cloud provider changes terms, fails, or leaves the jurisdiction?

### Privacy and rights

What information is collected, exposed, retained, or shared? Can people make
proportionate disclosures rather than exposing a full record by default?

### Inclusion and accessibility

Can people participate without a smartphone, continuous connectivity, high
technical literacy, or expensive hardware? What is the recovery path?

### Governance and accountability

Who can change rules? Who is accountable for application-level decisions? Which
parts are enforced technically, and which depend on policy or contracts?

### Exit and continuity

Can the institution retrieve records, verify claims, and migrate systems without
the supplier’s cooperation? Test the path, rather than relying on a promise.

## The capability requirement

Open infrastructure does not remove the need for institutional capability. A
public body that cannot specify, inspect, and govern its deployment may simply
recreate vendor dependency at a different layer.

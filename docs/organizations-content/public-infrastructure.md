# Digital public infrastructure with credible neutrality

**Route:** `/institutions/public-infrastructure`

## Hero

**H1:** Digital public infrastructure with credible neutrality

Identity, payments, records, and data exchange can become foundational public
infrastructure. When these systems rely on one operator, that operator can be a
single point of failure and control. Ethereum offers a public base layer whose
core rules are not owned by a vendor, agency, or company.

## What credible neutrality means

No organization, including the Ethereum Foundation, can unilaterally change the
core network rules to favor one participant. A public deployment still relies on
applications, integrators, and operators; assess them separately and design
alternatives and exit paths.

## Public infrastructure patterns

- **Credentials and identity:** allow people to hold and present verifiable
  claims while minimizing disclosure.
- **Records and registries:** create tamper-evident shared history where several
  parties need to verify data.
- **Payments and disbursements:** coordinate value transfers with clear rules
  and proportionate privacy.
- **Data exchange:** publish or prove information without requiring every
  verifier to depend on one database.

## What to weigh before committing

Plan for inclusion, accessibility, privacy, legal duties, key recovery, cost,
provider failure, and long-term in-house capability. If a public authority
legitimately controls a process end to end and no external party needs to verify
it independently, a well-governed conventional system may be better.

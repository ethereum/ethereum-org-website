# Evidence and case studies for institutions

**Route:** `/institutions/evidence-and-case-studies`

## Hero

**H1:** Evidence and case studies for institutions

This is a starting library for people assessing public-interest uses of Ethereum.
It separates documented implementations, reusable technical references, and open
questions so that a compelling story is never mistaken for proof.

## How to use this library

Start with the problem being addressed, the people affected, and the operating
context. Then examine a case study for its governance model, information flows,
maintenance responsibilities, and evidence of outcomes—not merely its choice of
technology.

## Documented implementations

Curate primary-source case studies in four groups:

- public registries and verifiable public records;
- credentials, certificates, and attestations;
- funding, grant, and public-goods mechanisms; and
- coordination tools used by civil-society networks.

Each entry should identify the operator, jurisdiction or community, maturity,
what data is onchain or offchain, the privacy model, dependencies, and the
source date. Do not feature an initiative solely because it uses Ethereum.

## Technical references

Link to the Ethereum protocol documentation, Ethereum Improvement Proposals,
standards for tokens and credentials, privacy research, and security guidance.
These references explain the tools; they do not validate a policy or a specific
deployment.

## Questions still open

Include work that reports constraints as well as successes: accessibility,
recourse, governance, cross-border operation, environmental assumptions,
interoperability, and long-term funding. Honest uncertainty is useful evidence.

## Submission and review policy

Proposed entries need a public primary source, enough information to describe
their system boundaries accurately, and periodic review. Listing is editorial
context, not endorsement, certification, or advice.

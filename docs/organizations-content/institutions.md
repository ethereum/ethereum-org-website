# Ethereum for public-interest institutions

**Route:** `/institutions`

## Hero

**H1:** Ethereum for public-interest institutions

Public agencies, multilateral organizations, civil-society groups, and NGOs can
use Ethereum to explore durable infrastructure for coordination, records,
payments, and credentials. The question is whether a shared public layer gives
people and institutions more independence, verifiability, and control than a
single operator can.

## Start with your mission

- **Public infrastructure:** identity, records, payments, data exchange, and
  long-lived civic systems.
- **Civil society and development:** public goods, transparent funding,
  humanitarian work, connectivity, and local coordination.
- **Policy and procurement:** sovereignty, inclusion, privacy, accountability,
  vendor dependence, and exit paths.
- **Evidence:** primary-source case studies and research to assess in context.

## A public-interest standard

Technology does not make a service legitimate by itself. Public-interest
deployment should protect privacy, accessibility, agency, and recourse; be
understandable enough to govern; and remain usable if an integrator, provider,
or funder changes course.

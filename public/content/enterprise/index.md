Ethereum is public infrastructure that no company owns and no company can switch off. That can give an organization a more open, interoperable, and verifiable foundation for coordination—but it also changes how the organization manages privacy, security, operations, and risk.

This hub explains what Ethereum provides, what it does not provide, and how to evaluate whether it fits your organization.

## A foundation, not a finished product {#a-foundation-not-a-finished-product}

Ethereum does not provide legal compliance, custody, incident response, or a business model. Public activity is observable unless privacy is designed in. Smart contracts, keys, bridges, providers, and upgrades all introduce risks of their own. Responsible adoption starts with one bounded use case and a clear exit path for users and the organization.
